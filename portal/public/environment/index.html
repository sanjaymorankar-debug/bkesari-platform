Four figures worth knowing

+0.7 °C Rise in India's average temperature, 1901 to 2018 Ministry of Earth Sciences, Assessment of Climate Change over the Indian Region, 2020
~420 ppm Carbon dioxide in the atmosphere, against roughly 280 ppm before industrialisation NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory, 2023 annual mean. Rises about 2–3 ppm a year, so check the current value
3.4 mm Sea level rise per year, averaged over the satellite record NASA Sea Level Change Portal, satellite altimetry since 1993
~7% Extra water vapour air can hold per 1 °C of warming — why heavy rain gets heavier Clausius–Clapeyron relation; IPCC AR6 Working Group I, Chapter 8, 2021

Why the last figure matters most here

Warmer air holds more moisture. That does not spread evenly as gentle rain — it concentrates. The same season can bring a longer dry spell and a heavier downpour when the rain finally arrives, which is the pattern Indian monsoon records increasingly show.

For anyone who depends on a predictable season — a dairy farmer planning fodder, a shopkeeper planning stock, a family planning a harvest — the difficulty is less the annual average than the loss of predictability around it.

What this means day to day

  • Heat. More days above the threshold at which outdoor work becomes unsafe, and warmer nights, which is when the body recovers from daytime heat.
  • Water. Rain arriving in fewer, heavier bursts runs off instead of soaking in, so the same total rainfall recharges less groundwater.
  • Livestock and crops. Milk yields fall under heat stress, and both flowering and harvest windows shift.
  • Coasts. A higher baseline sea level means an ordinary storm surge reaches further inland than the same surge once did.

What actually reduces it

Warming tracks the total carbon dioxide ever emitted, not the rate in any one year. That is why the measures below are ordered by how much they remove, rather than by how visible they are.

  • Electricity without combustion. Solar and wind displace the largest single source of emissions.
  • Electrifying what burns fuel directly — vehicles, cooking, industrial heat — which only helps as fast as the grid behind it cleans up.
  • Using less to begin with. Efficiency and reduced waste cut emissions without needing anything new to be built.
  • Protecting what already stores carbon: forests, soil and wetlands.
