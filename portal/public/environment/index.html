Why the last figure matters most here

Warmer air holds more moisture. That does not spread evenly as gentle rain — it concentrates. The same season can bring a longer dry spell and a heavier downpour when the rain finally arrives, which is the pattern Indian monsoon records increasingly show.

For anyone who depends on a predictable season — a dairy farmer planning fodder, a shopkeeper planning stock, a family planning a harvest — the difficulty is less the annual average than the loss of predictability around it.

Longer dry spells and heavier downpours, in the same season.

What this means day to day

  • Heat

    More days above the threshold at which outdoor work becomes unsafe, and warmer nights, which is when the body recovers from daytime heat.

  • Water

    Rain arriving in fewer, heavier bursts runs off instead of soaking in, so the same total rainfall recharges less groundwater.

  • Livestock and crops

    Milk yields fall under heat stress, and both flowering and harvest windows shift.

  • Coasts

    A higher baseline sea level means an ordinary storm surge reaches further inland than the same surge once did.

What actually reduces it

Warming tracks the total carbon dioxide ever emitted, not the rate in any one year. That is why the measures below are ordered by how much they remove, rather than by how visible they are.

  1. Electricity without combustion

    Solar and wind displace the largest single source of emissions.

  2. Electrifying what burns fuel directly

    Vehicles, cooking, industrial heat — which only helps as fast as the grid behind it cleans up.

  3. Using less to begin with

    Efficiency and reduced waste cut emissions without needing anything new to be built.

  4. Protecting what already stores carbon

    Forests, soil and wetlands.

Activities you can start this month

For a household, a school or a village. Each one answers one of the effects above.

  • Catch the rain

    Rooftop rainwater harvesting and recharge pits put heavy bursts of rain back into the ground instead of the drain.

    Answers: water

  • Plant native trees, and look after them

    Planted before the monsoon and watered through their first summers, they store carbon and shade homes and cattle.

    Answers: heat and carbon

  • Switch to solar where you can

    Rooftop panels and solar pumps replace electricity from combustion, the largest single source of emissions.

    Answers: carbon

  • Use less, waste less

    Efficient fans and lights, fixed leaks and less waste cut emissions without anything new being built.

    Answers: carbon

  • Protect ponds, wetlands and soil

    Kept healthy, village ponds, wetlands, grazing land and soil go on holding both carbon and water.

    Answers: water and carbon

  • Keep people and animals safe in the heat

    Shade and drinking water for outdoor workers and cattle, and heavy work moved to the cooler hours on the hottest days.

    Answers: heat
